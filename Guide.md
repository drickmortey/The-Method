## Sup
I'm the one who writes all this

I mainly classify programming in game development into three categories:

1. Language
2. Engine API
3. Math

When learning, it's crucial to have all of these balanced. If you focus too much on one of these, you'll know a lot yet still be unable to make anything cool, substantial or put any of your ambitions into effect. I'm an example, I used to go all in on the language, ended up knowing a fair lot, and couldn't do much outside of writing basic-to-intermediate programs in fancy designs and paradigms..

## Language
Before anything at all, you need to know how to write code. Know how code works, and why. This is foundational, about as important as water. Though it is not king over the others of its kind. A pizza without cheese or sauce is just bread. Also linguists are boring.

## Engine API
This generally has a pretty broad meaning, so I'll define this as *whatever the Roblox engine gives the programmer to control and access it*. Basically, without Engine API, you can write crazy algorithms and solve a million dollar problem but still have your game world be a sad empty baseplate since you need to interact with the Roblox engine to cause any effect there (with scripts).

## Math
How do you get the distance between two objects (spoiler: vectors)? How do you get the direction from point A to point B (spoiler: vectors)? How do you give orientation to a part (spoiler: vectors)? How do you change the size or position of something, or where it's looking? How do you do anything at all?

If this already wasn't music to your ears, game dev math is 80% vector math. If you have to calculate something, 8 times out of 10 you're going to use that in a vector in some capacity (even unknowingly). Calculated sine and cosine? The next line definitely plugs that into a vector...

You learn about vectors from a mathematical sense in high school. You learn about vectors straight after the front door in Roblox. You can know all the API.. but like half of the ones you'll use the most take a vector. Yeah math is pretty important.

Want to make something as basic as a camera shake? Or even have the camera follow another player? To the innocent mind, the underlying math looks like lightning from the fingertips. To the enlightened mind, 'tis just vector.

Anyway I just really want to emphasize math in scripting (and how everything is jus vector 🥲).

This should give you an idea on what your individual priorities should be when deciding what to go through in the course. No this is not a psyop.
